Abstract

Getting the right answer isn’t the same as being coherent.

Current LLM evaluation measures whether models answer individual questions correctly. We argue this is structurally incomplete: logical consistency — the invariance of a model’s responses under truth-preserving logical transformations — is a behaviorally independent property that single-question benchmarks cannot measure by construction. We formalize this through a theory of constraint-preserving transformations, derive a principled taxonomy of five transformation families from first principles, and introduce ConsistencyBench, a framework for measuring consistency as a first-class evaluation axis.

ConsistencyBench comprises a formal theory grounded in operator-theoretic definitions; ProbeGen, a four-stage constraint-driven probe synthesis pipeline that difficulty-calibrates probes through semantic distance maximization rather than ad-hoc prompting; a dataset of 4,500 probe pairs across five families, three domains, and three difficulty levels; a novel Consistency Profile that gives every model a multi-dimensional fingerprint rather than a single score; and an empirical evaluation of 17 frontier models spanning closed-source, open-weight, and reasoning-specialized paradigms.

01 · Why single-question benchmarks miss this

Accuracy and consistency are two independent axes

Every major benchmark asks: given a question, is the answer right? That is a property of one response. Consistency is a property of the relationship between two responses to logically related prompts — it cannot be seen by asking one question at a time, no matter how many questions you ask.

Accuracy
Does M(q) = a*? A pointwise property of one prompt→response mapping. This is what MMLU, GSM8K, MATH, and HELM measure.
Consistency
Does Cᵤ(M(p), M(T(p))) = ⊤? A property of the pair — holds or fails regardless of whether either answer is true.

Across the 16 models with published MMLU scores, Kendall’s τ = −0.32 (p = 0.076, n.s.) between accuracy and inconsistency rate. Spearman’s ρ = −0.49 reaches significance (p = 0.048); an OLS fit does not (p = 0.123). The honest reading: a modest negative trend that is not robust across tests — accuracy is largely, but not perfectly, independent of consistency.

02 · Theory

Five transformation families, derived from first principles

Not a list of intuitive failure modes — the five families are the operations on propositions and binary relations from classical logic and relation theory that are truth-preserving, expressible as natural-language prompt pairs, and verifiable without full semantic parsing. Together they cover 83.4% of 500 annotated real-world LLM logical failures.

Composition

Relation composition (transitivity)
A⇒B, B⇒C ⇒ A⇒C
pᵀ establishes A⇒B
pᵇ queries A⇒C, given B⇒C
Coverage18.4%

Reversal

Symmetric relation reversal
rel(X,Y) ⇔ rel(Y,X)
pᵀ queries rel(X, Y)
pᵇ queries rel(Y, X)
Coverage14.2%

Complement

Truth complement (negation)
¬(assert(P) ∧ assert(¬P))
pᵀ asserts P
pᵇ queries ¬P (equivalent negation)
Coverage28.6%

Ordering

Asymmetric temporal ordering
before(A,B) ⇔ after(B,A)
pᵀ queries before(A, B)
pᵇ queries after(B, A)
Coverage10.8%

Equivalence

Semantic equivalence preservation
equiv(pᵀ,pᵇ) ⇒ compat(rᵀ,rᵇ)
pᵀ surface form 1 of φ
pᵇ surface form 2 (distance-maximized)
Coverage11.4%
03 · Method

ProbeGen: constraint-driven probe synthesis

Difficulty is not an arbitrary label — it is δ = 0.4·dlex + 0.4·dsem + 0.2·dsyn, a measurable semantic distance between prompt A and prompt B, targeted directly during generation and re-verified afterward.

STAGE 1

Constraint Specification

Pure logic, no LLM call. A constraint graph G = (V, E, C) is built from the transformation family, domain, and target difficulty.

STAGE 2

Semantic Instantiation

Gemini 2.5 Pro (excluded from evaluation) instantiates the graph into two standalone, self-contained natural-language prompts.

STAGE 3

Difficulty Calibration

Maximizes lexical & semantic distance δ between prompt A and B while the logical constraint is preserved.

STAGE 4

Constraint Verification

5 automated checks (validity, preservation, type, scorability, dedup). Failing probes are rejected & regenerated — 4.2% overall rejection rate.

Human validation on 300 sampled probes: Cohen’s κ = 0.81, Krippendorff’s α = 0.79. Per-family agreement highest for Ordering (94.2%), lowest for Equivalence (79.3%). Cross-judge robustness (re-scoring 400 LJS probes with GPT-4o as an alternative judge): κ = 0.86.

04 · Results

Seven findings from 17 models × 4,500 probe pairs

Reported candidly, including a negative result and the limitations of each claim — see the paper for full statistical detail.

01

Consistency failures are widespread but moderate mean IR 9.0%

Overall IR ranges from 4.2% (Claude 4.5 Opus, best) to 14.2% (GPT-4o mini, worst), mean 9.0%, median 9.1%. Every model fails on at least 1-in-24 probe pairs; the best-to-worst spread exceeds 3×. A χ² test confirms IR is non-uniform across models (χ²(16)=106.8, p=1.85×10⁻¹⁵).

02

IR’s relation to accuracy is weak and test-dependent τ=−0.32, n.s.

Kendall’s τ = −0.32 (p=0.076) does not reach significance; Spearman’s ρ = −0.49 (p=0.048) does; an OLS fit gives r = −0.39 (p=0.123, n.s.). The sign is consistently negative, but not robust across tests — outliers like Phi-4 (moderate MMLU, only middling IR) keep the relationship well short of predictive. Accuracy alone is not a reliable proxy for consistency. See the chart in §01 above.

03

Family difficulty is moderately consistent across models mean ρ=0.57

Averaged across all 17 models: Equivalence (12.8%) > Reversal (11.8%) > Complement (7.0%) > Ordering (2.3%) > Composition (1.5%). Paraphrase-based and reordering probes are hardest; simple conjunctions and element reordering are comparatively easy. The ranking is directional, not rigid — pairwise Spearman correlation between any two models’ 5-family IR vectors ranges 0.05–1.00 across 136 model pairs. Qwen3-235B (40% IR on Equivalence alone) deviates sharply from the population trend.

04

No robust family-specific effect of reasoning training negative result

Two architecture-matched pairs (DeepSeek R1 vs. V3; o4-mini vs. GPT-4o mini) disagree in sign on three of five families. A group-level test (3 reasoning-labeled models vs. 14 others) shows no significant difference in any family (Mann-Whitney p>0.14 throughout). Claims that reasoning training selectively fixes chain-following failure types are not supported by this dataset.

05

Ethics domain exposes the deepest inconsistency +14.4pp, p<10⁻⁶

Ethics probes show higher IR than science probes for every one of the 17 models, mean gap 14.4 percentage points (range 8.4–18.8pp). Paired t=18.9, p<10⁻⁶; one-sided Wilcoxon agrees (p<0.0001). The largest and most statistically robust effect in the study — attributed to the absence of stable internal representations for normative claims, unlike factual science content.

06

Consistency Profiles reveal model-specific structure IR↔CCS τ=0.007, n.s.

The aggregate IR number hides real structure: Qwen3-235B spikes to 40% IR on Equivalence alone despite 9.3% overall IR; GPT-4o mini is broadly elevated across Reversal, Complement, and Ordering; Claude 4.5 Opus and Sonnet are the only two models with 0% IR on more than one family simultaneously. IR and CCS (whether a model’s confidence predicts its own inconsistency) are statistically independent — two separate reliability axes, not two views of one number.

07

Plain Self-Check beats the fancier targeted variant −78% relative, best case

Self-Check (show the model its own prior answer) — not Family-Targeted Self-Check — gave the largest reductions observed: −78.3% relative for DeepSeek R1, −58.7% for Llama 4 Maverick. FTSC recovers only part of that gain. GPT-4o was not helped by any intervention tested. Tested on only 3 models — read as “true for these three,” not yet a general claim.

07 · Reported candidly

Limitations

  • Intervention coverage. Only 3 of 17 models were tested under intervention (DeepSeek R1, GPT-4o, Llama 4 Maverick). The SC-over-FTSC result is true for these three, not yet a general claim.
  • Reasoning-training group size. The reasoning-vs-non-reasoning comparison has n=3 in the reasoning group. A true moderate effect would likely fail to reach significance at this size — the honest reading is “no effect detected,” not “no effect exists.”
  • Equivalence family has the lowest inter-annotator agreement (79.3%) — it requires semantic judgment rather than a purely rule-based check.
  • Difficulty-stratified analysis is not yet included pending the raw per-probe difficulty export needed to reproduce it honestly.
  • Probes are LLM-generated (mitigated by a separate, excluded generator model, human validation, and cross-judge robustness checks) — not sourced from real user data.
  • English-only in the current release; multilingual probes, multi-hop chains, and modal/probabilistic reasoning are noted as future work.
